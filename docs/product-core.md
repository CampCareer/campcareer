# CampCareer Product Core

## The permanent job to be done

CampCareer answers one question:

> How can a person with citizenship in country A pursue a specific occupation or industry C in country B?

The answer is a source-backed route, not a country ranking, a generic study guide, or a productivity workspace.

## Required route answer

Every published route must identify the person's citizenship, destination, and occupation or industry. It must provide:

1. Viable visa or legal-entry options, with official sources and a checked date.
2. Eligibility checkpoints and visa conditions. Never present these as an approval decision.
3. Work that is permitted, including material limits that change how a person can use the route.
4. A practical preparation sequence and role-specific credential or course links.
5. Live job-search links and a map of relevant regions or employers when evidence exists.
6. Clear uncertainty. A missing, stale, or non-exact item is unavailable, not estimated.

## Publication rule

Do not publish a route until its visa, work-condition, job-search, preparation, and map outputs have current sources. A route can be added one corridor at a time. Broad country rankings and inferred eligibility are prohibited.

## Allowed product surfaces

- Route search
- A route result page
- Maps used to explore the selected destination and work regions
- Methodology and legal pages

The following are outside the product core: planners, dashboards, onboarding funnels, generic wizards, applications trackers, budget tools, English trackers, country rankings, university comparison, and engagement features.

## Monetisation sequence

1. First earn trust and prove demand through completed route searches, official-source clicks, job-link clicks, and map exploration.
2. Add clearly labelled, route-relevant affiliate links for courses, insurance, or relocation only when they do not influence route eligibility or ranking.
3. Offer a paid human route review only after enough users request a verified route that is not published.
4. Do not sell user contact details or place paid providers ahead of official evidence.

The primary success metric is a completed route search that leads to a useful official, job, course, or map click. Visitor volume is not the product metric.

## Scope decision

When a proposed feature does not make the answer to A -> C -> B more complete, more current, or easier to act on, reject it. If the route evidence is not ready, improve the evidence rather than adding another feature.
