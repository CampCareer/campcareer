Among the eight Australian careers that have passed CampCareer’s current public evidence review, **Registered Nurse ranks highest at 82/100**. **Electrician and Care Worker are next at 78/100**, but for very different reasons: Electrician combines high demand with stronger pay and a harder entry path, while Care Worker combines high demand with easier entry and more moderate pay.

This is **not a ranking of every career in Australia**, and it is not a list where every job has high pay. It compares only careers for which CampCareer currently has enough reviewed evidence to publish a Demand, Pay and Entry score. The goal is to show the trade-off that generic “highest-paying jobs” lists usually miss.

## The quick answer: which careers offer the best balance?

If you want the strongest overall balance of demand, pay and entry in CampCareer’s current reviewed Australian set, **Registered Nurse is first at 82/100**. It has exceptional demand and strong earnings, but the entry path requires an approved nursing qualification, clinical training and professional registration.

If you want a **high-paying trade without a university degree**, **Electrician is the standout at 78/100**. Its official occupation-group earnings are almost identical to Registered Nurse on the current weekly median, but the pathway still involves a multi-year apprenticeship, Certificate III training and state or territory licensing.

If you care most about **easier entry while retaining strong demand**, **Care Worker also scores 78/100**. Its Entry score is 9/10, the highest in this group, but Pay is 5/10. That is exactly why one-dimensional salary rankings can be misleading.

## Why demand still matters in Australia in 2026

Australia’s labour market is not in a universal worker shortage. Jobs and Skills Australia’s 2025 Occupation Shortage List found **29% of assessed occupations in shortage**, down from 33% in 2024 and 36% in 2023. But the remaining shortages are concentrated: nearly half of trade roles and about two in five professional occupations were still in shortage, particularly in health, education and construction.

The labour market also changes quickly. In the March 2026 Occupation Shortage Report, the national vacancy fill rate fell to **68.2%**, with recruitment conditions weakening across metropolitan and regional Australia. This is why CampCareer does not treat a single shortage label as enough evidence on its own.

CampCareer’s Demand dimension combines reviewed shortage, vacancy and employment-growth evidence where available. The current public score is intentionally narrower than a claim such as “this job is guaranteed to be in demand everywhere.”

## 1. Registered Nurse: the strongest overall balance in the current set

[Registered Nurse in Australia](/career/australia/registered-nurse) has the highest current CampCareer Score at **82/100: Demand 10, Pay 8, Entry 6**.

The current Jobs and Skills Australia earnings benchmark is **A$2,192 per week before tax** for full-time Registered Nurses. CampCareer’s reviewed profile also records Registered Nurse in shortage across every state and territory in the 2025 Occupation Shortage List, with strong vacancy and longer-term employment-growth evidence.

The catch is entry. Nursing is not a low-barrier career in the casual sense. Entry normally requires an approved nursing program, substantial clinical placement and registration with the Nursing and Midwifery Board of Australia through Ahpra.

That makes Registered Nurse a strong option for someone willing to invest in a regulated professional pathway, not someone looking for the shortest possible route into work.

- [Jobs and Skills Australia: Registered Nurses](https://www.jobsandskills.gov.au/data/occupation-and-industry-profiles/occupations/2544-registered-nurses)
- [Nursing and Midwifery Board of Australia: Registration](https://www.nursingmidwiferyboard.gov.au/Registration-Standards.aspx)

## 2. Electrician: the strongest high-paying trade in this comparison

[Electrician in Australia](/career/australia/electrician) scores **78/100: Demand 9, Pay 8, Entry 6**.

The official Jobs and Skills Australia profile publishes **A$2,191 in median full-time weekly earnings** and **A$55 median hourly earnings** for the broader Electricians occupation group. That makes the national earnings signal almost identical to Registered Nurse in this comparison.

Electrician is also a useful example of why “no degree required” does not mean “easy to enter.” The standard Electrician (General) route involves the UEE30820 Certificate III in Electrotechnology Electrician, supervised workplace training or an apprenticeship, and an electrical licence or certificate under state or territory rules.

For people who want a technical trade and prefer apprenticeship-based training to university, the balance is attractive. For someone who wants immediate unsupervised work after a short course, it is not.

- [Jobs and Skills Australia: Electricians](https://www.jobsandskills.gov.au/data/occupation-and-industry-profiles/occupations/3411-electricians)
- [Training.gov.au: UEE30820 Certificate III in Electrotechnology Electrician](https://training.gov.au/training/details/UEE30820)

## 3. Care Worker: strongest entry accessibility, with a pay trade-off

[Care Worker in Australia](/career/australia/care-worker) also scores **78/100**, but its components are very different: **Demand 9, Pay 5, Entry 9**.

The current labour-market benchmark used in the profile is **A$1,761 per week before tax** for the broader Aged and Disabled Carers series. CampCareer’s exact Care Worker career scope is narrower than that legacy labour-market group, so the page makes the scope difference explicit.

The attraction is the entry path. There is no single national occupational licence for the whole career scope, although screening, employer and service-specific requirements can apply. CHC33021 Certificate III in Individual Support is one structured route into ageing or disability support work.

Care Worker therefore fits a different user than Electrician. It offers a more accessible route into a high-demand field, but it does not offer the same national median pay signal.

- [Jobs and Skills Australia: Aged and Disabled Carers](https://www.jobsandskills.gov.au/data/occupation-and-industry-profiles/occupations/4231-aged-and-disabled-carers)
- [Training.gov.au: CHC33021 Certificate III in Individual Support](https://training.gov.au/training/details/CHC33021)

## 4. Midwife: strong pay and demand, but a specialised regulated path

[Midwife in Australia](/career/australia/midwife) scores **75/100: Demand 9, Pay 7, Entry 6**.

The current full-time weekly earnings benchmark is **A$2,114 before tax**. The profile also records national and all-state-and-territory shortage evidence in the 2025 OSL.

Entry is specialised. Midwives need an approved entry-to-practice qualification and registration with the Nursing and Midwifery Board of Australia. Graduate-entry pathways for people who are already Registered Nurses are a separate route and should not be confused with direct-entry midwifery programs.

This is a strong career for someone who actually wants maternity care and accepts the professional training burden. High demand alone is not a reason to choose a clinical profession.

- [Jobs and Skills Australia: Midwives](https://www.jobsandskills.gov.au/data/occupation-and-industry-profiles/occupations/2541-midwives)
- [Nursing and Midwifery Board of Australia](https://www.nursingmidwiferyboard.gov.au/)

## 5. Physiotherapist: very strong demand, more moderate relative pay

[Physiotherapist in Australia](/career/australia/physiotherapist) scores **69/100: Demand 9, Pay 5, Entry 6**.

The current national full-time earnings benchmark is **A$1,888 per week before tax**. Demand is the stronger part of the profile: the reviewed evidence includes shortage across every state and territory in the 2025 OSL and substantial longer-term employment growth.

The entry burden is meaningful. Physiotherapists need an approved entry-to-practice qualification, clinical education and registration with the Physiotherapy Board of Australia through Ahpra.

For a prospective student, that means the real comparison is not just “physio salary versus another salary.” It is the return on several years of professional education against the demand and earnings available after registration.

- [Jobs and Skills Australia: Physiotherapists](https://www.jobsandskills.gov.au/data/occupation-and-industry-profiles/occupations/2525-physiotherapists)
- [Physiotherapy Board of Australia: Registration standards](https://www.physiotherapyboard.gov.au/Registration-Standards.aspx)

## 6. Carpenter: a trade route with manageable entry, but not a salary outlier

[Carpenter in Australia](/career/australia/carpenter) scores **68/100: Demand 8, Pay 5, Entry 7**.

The current full-time weekly earnings benchmark is **A$1,760 before tax**. CampCareer’s reviewed profile combines shortage evidence with vacancy and employment-growth signals, while treating the trade-entry burden separately.

Carpentry does not normally require a university degree, but the standard route is still structured around an apprenticeship and CPC30220 Certificate III in Carpentry. Construction induction and licensing or registration requirements can vary by state, territory and the type of work undertaken.

That makes Carpenter a potentially realistic trade pathway, but not a “quick high-paying job with no qualifications.”

- [Jobs and Skills Australia: Carpenters and Joiners](https://www.jobsandskills.gov.au/data/occupation-and-industry-profiles/occupations/3312-carpenters-and-joiners)
- [Training.gov.au: CPC30220 Certificate III in Carpentry](https://training.gov.au/training/details/CPC30220)

## 7. Occupational Therapist: useful demand, but the education investment matters

[Occupational Therapist in Australia](/career/australia/occupational-therapist) scores **65/100: Demand 8, Pay 5, Entry 6**.

The current full-time weekly earnings benchmark is **A$1,913 before tax**. The profile records positive shortage and growth evidence, while the Pay score remains more moderate relative to the education and registration burden.

Occupational Therapists need a registration-qualifying higher education program, professional practice education and registration with the Occupational Therapy Board of Australia through Ahpra.

This is exactly the kind of career where Camp and Career should eventually connect: a student should compare the cost of qualifying programs against the actual career outcome, rather than choosing a university first and asking about the job later.

- [Jobs and Skills Australia: Occupational Therapists](https://www.jobsandskills.gov.au/data/occupation-and-industry-profiles/occupations/2524-occupational-therapists)
- [Occupational Therapy Board of Australia: Registration](https://www.occupationaltherapyboard.gov.au/Registration.aspx)

## 8. Welder: shortage demand and a trade pathway, with lower median pay

[Welder in Australia](/career/australia/welder) scores **65/100: Demand 8, Pay 4, Entry 7**.

The reviewed profile uses the JSA Structural Steel and Welding Trades Workers series, which aligns with CampCareer’s Metal Fabricator, Pressure Welder and Welder (First Class) roll-up. The current full-time weekly earnings benchmark is **A$1,688 before tax**, below the all-occupation median used in the same evidence set.

Demand is the stronger side of the story. All three mapped occupations are recorded in national shortage in the 2025 OSL, although state-level patterns differ for Pressure Welder.

The standard route is a multi-year fabrication apprenticeship and Certificate III trade training. There is no single national welder licence, but construction-site, safety, employer and task-specific requirements can apply.

- [Jobs and Skills Australia: Structural Steel and Welding Trades Workers](https://www.jobsandskills.gov.au/data/occupation-and-industry-profiles/occupations-anzsco/3223-structural-steel-and-welding-trades-workers)
- [Training.gov.au: MEM31925 Certificate III in Engineering - Fabrication Trade](https://training.gov.au/Training/Details/MEM31925)

## What does “low entry barrier” actually mean?

A high Entry score does **not** mean a career is easy, safe for everyone or available immediately.

CampCareer’s Entry dimension is designed to compare the practical burden of getting into a career. Depending on the occupation, that burden can include:

- years of required education or training
- apprenticeship or supervised-work requirements
- professional registration or occupational licensing
- clinical placement or workplace competency requirements
- mandatory screening, safety or induction requirements
- whether a recognised path exists for a new entrant

This is why Care Worker can score Entry 9 while Electrician scores Entry 6. Both can be viable non-traditional paths, but an electrician must cross a longer regulated training and licensing route before independent electrical work.

For the full scoring definition, see [CampCareer methodology](/methodology).

## High demand does not guarantee you a job

An occupation being in shortage is evidence about employer recruitment difficulty, not a promise that every applicant will be hired.

Actual outcomes depend on location, experience, qualification recognition, employer requirements, communication skills, work rights, licensing and the specific job market when you apply. A national shortage can also coexist with weaker demand in a particular state, speciality or experience level.

Use Demand as one decision input. Then verify the pathway and current employer requirements on the individual Career Page.

## Visa and PR are not part of the CampCareer Score

CampCareer deliberately keeps visa eligibility, permanent residence and personal work rights outside the public Career Score.

A high-demand occupation can appear on an immigration-related occupation list without making an individual eligible for a visa. Likewise, a strong CampCareer Score does not mean a career is an “easy PR pathway.”

The Score answers a narrower question: **how does this career currently look on Demand, Pay and Entry?** Personal immigration eligibility must be checked separately against current Department of Home Affairs rules.

## How to choose between these careers

Start with the trade-off you are willing to accept rather than the headline score alone.

If your priority is the **strongest overall evidence balance**, start with [Registered Nurse](/career/australia/registered-nurse). If you want a **high-paying technical trade without university**, inspect [Electrician](/career/australia/electrician). If you want **strong demand with a relatively accessible entry path**, compare [Care Worker](/career/australia/care-worker). If you want a **trade route with manageable entry**, compare [Carpenter](/career/australia/carpenter) and [Welder](/career/australia/welder).

For health professions such as [Midwife](/career/australia/midwife), [Physiotherapist](/career/australia/physiotherapist) and [Occupational Therapist](/career/australia/occupational-therapist), the bigger question is whether the regulated education investment is worth the career outcome for you.

The sequence should be:

1. Choose the career you would actually want to do.
2. Check Demand, Pay and Entry evidence.
3. Understand the qualification, apprenticeship or registration path.
4. Compare only programs that lead toward that career.
5. Validate the current job market and employer requirements.
6. Check personal visa and work-right conditions separately if they apply to you.

## Frequently asked questions

**What careers are in high demand in Australia in 2026?**

There is no single list of careers that are high demand everywhere. In CampCareer’s current evidence-reviewed Australian set, Registered Nurse has Demand 10/10; Electrician, Care Worker, Midwife and Physiotherapist have Demand 9/10; Carpenter, Occupational Therapist and Welder have Demand 8/10. These scores use reviewed shortage, vacancy and growth evidence rather than a shortage label alone.

**What is the highest-scoring career in Australia on CampCareer right now?**

Among the careers currently approved for a public CampCareer Score in Australia, Registered Nurse is highest at 82/100. This is a limited evidence-reviewed set, not a ranking of every occupation in Australia.

**What high-paying career in Australia does not require a university degree?**

Electrician is the strongest example in this comparison. Jobs and Skills Australia publishes median full-time earnings of A$2,191 per week for the Electricians occupation group. The standard pathway does not require university, but it does require trade training, supervised workplace experience and licensing.

**Which career has the easiest entry path in this comparison?**

Care Worker has the highest current Entry score at 9/10. That reflects a more accessible pathway relative to the other careers in this set, not an absence of screening, training or employer requirements.

**Does an occupation shortage guarantee a job?**

No. A shortage indicates recruitment difficulty in the labour market under the methodology used by Jobs and Skills Australia. It does not guarantee employment for an individual applicant or in every location.

**Does a high CampCareer Score mean I can get an Australian visa or permanent residence?**

No. Visa, work rights and personal eligibility are outside the public CampCareer Score. Check current Department of Home Affairs requirements separately.

## Sources and methodology

This guide uses CampCareer’s public, source-reviewed Australia Career profiles as of 15 August 2026. Underlying evidence includes Jobs and Skills Australia occupation profiles, the Occupation Shortage List, the Internet Vacancy Index, employment projections, ABS occupation classifications, Training.gov.au qualifications and relevant Australian professional or occupational regulators.

CampCareer Score v1 is:

**Demand 40% + Pay 30% + Entry 30%**

or, on a 100-point scale:

**Demand × 4 + Pay × 3 + Entry × 3**

The Score is a career-evidence tool, not a government rating, salary promise, hiring forecast for an individual, or immigration assessment.
