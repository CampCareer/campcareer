The figures come from the ABS Survey of Employee Earnings and Hours and are published for the broader ANZSCO 3411 Electricians occupation group, not as a guaranteed salary for every individual Electrician (General).

## Electrician salary in Australia: the quick answer

The strongest official national benchmark currently available on the Jobs and Skills Australia occupation page is **A$2,191 per week before tax for full-time Electricians**, compared with **A$1,852 across all occupations**. Median hourly earnings are **A$55**, compared with **A$47 across all occupations**.

That does not mean every electrician earns A$55 an hour. Jobs and Skills Australia states that its median earnings measure covers full-time, non-managerial adult-rate employees and does not account for experience or other factors that affect individual wages. The published figure is also for the ANZSCO 3411 group, which includes Electricians (General), Electricians (Special Class) and Lift Mechanics.

For a career decision, use the number as a national earnings benchmark rather than a promised wage.

## What does the A$2,191 weekly figure actually measure?

Salary articles often mix four different numbers: official median earnings, award minimums, advertised job salaries and contractor revenue. They are not interchangeable.

Jobs and Skills Australia publishes **median full-time earnings before tax** for the occupation group using customised ABS data. This is the middle of the measured full-time earnings distribution, not an entry-level rate and not a legal minimum.

An advertised salary on a job board is different again. It reflects the vacancies being advertised at that moment and can be skewed by industry, location, overtime, allowances or seniority. A contractor’s business revenue is also not the same as the electrician’s personal wage because business costs must be paid from that revenue.

When comparing careers, CampCareer therefore treats the official earnings benchmark as evidence rather than converting one headline into a promise about your future income.

## Why electrician pay can vary

Individual electrician earnings can differ substantially from the national median. The main reasons include:

- experience and responsibility level
- employee versus contractor status
- ordinary hours versus overtime or shift work
- industrial, commercial, residential or infrastructure work
- specialist licences or competencies
- state and territory labour conditions
- remote or project-based work

The official national figure is useful because it gives a consistent comparison point. It should not be used to predict a precise offer in Sydney, Perth, Brisbane or a regional project without local vacancy evidence.

## How do you become an electrician in Australia?

The national training register lists **UEE30820 Certificate III in Electrotechnology Electrician** as a current Certificate III qualification mapped to **ANZSCO 341111 Electrician (General)**. The qualification contains the electrotechnology competencies used in the trade pathway and is delivered by registered training organisations that hold it on scope.

The qualification alone should not be treated as permission to perform unsupervised electrical work everywhere in Australia. Electrical work is regulated by states and territories.

For example, Service NSW states that a person must hold an electrical licence or certificate to perform electrical wiring work in New South Wales. It also states that apprentices or trainees cannot apply for the completed licence or certificate until they finish their apprenticeship or traineeship. In Queensland, WorkSafe states that electrical work without the appropriate licence is illegal except for limited authorised circumstances, including supervised learning as an apprentice or trainee.

A practical pathway is therefore:

1. Confirm the Electrician (General) trade is the actual occupation you want, rather than choosing a course title first.
2. Check the current UEE30820 qualification and the registered training organisations approved to deliver it.
3. Secure the workplace training or apprenticeship arrangement required for the trade pathway applicable to you.
4. Complete the required competencies and supervised practical training.
5. Apply to the relevant state or territory authority for the electrical work licence or certificate required for the work you intend to perform.
6. Check any additional contractor licensing requirements separately if you plan to contract directly to customers or operate a business.

<Callout type="warning" title="Training and licensing are separate checks">
A nationally recognised qualification does not automatically give unrestricted permission to perform electrical work in every state or territory. Verify the regulator, licence class and eligibility rules where you intend to work.
</Callout>

## Do you need university to become an electrician?

The standard Electrician (General) pathway is a **trade qualification route, not a university-degree route**. Training.gov.au classifies UEE30820 as Certificate III and maps it to Electrician (General).

That does not make entry easy. The value of the trade qualification comes from combining technical learning with workplace competency and licensing. Anyone comparing Electrician with an electrical-engineering degree should treat them as different occupations with different education, registration and job scopes.

## Is electrician a good career in Australia?

On CampCareer’s current reviewed evidence, Electrician is a **Strong** Australian career at **78/100**. Demand and Pay are the main strengths. Entry is the constraint because the route requires more than simply completing classroom study.

Whether it is a good career for you depends on factors the public Score does not attempt to decide, including your aptitude for technical and hands-on work, safety responsibility, willingness to complete supervised trade training, physical work preferences and your personal right to study and work in Australia.

The correct sequence is therefore **career first, pathway second**. Decide whether you want the actual work of an electrician, then investigate the qualification, apprenticeship and licence that lead to it.

## What should international students check?

International students should separate four questions that are often collapsed into one:

1. Is the course available to international students at the provider and location you are considering?
2. Can you obtain the workplace training or apprenticeship experience required for the trade pathway?
3. Will the completed training satisfy the relevant electrical licensing authority’s requirements?
4. Do your visa and work conditions independently allow the study and work arrangements involved?

A course advertisement cannot answer all four. Verify each requirement with the training provider, the relevant state or territory electrical regulator and the Department of Home Affairs before making a financial commitment.

## Frequently asked questions

**How much do electricians earn per hour in Australia?**

Jobs and Skills Australia publishes median hourly earnings of A$55 for the ANZSCO 3411 Electricians group. It is a national occupation-group median, not a guaranteed hourly rate for every Electrician (General).

**What is the median weekly electrician salary in Australia?**

The current Jobs and Skills Australia occupation profile publishes A$2,191 in median full-time weekly earnings before tax for Electricians, compared with A$1,852 for all occupations.

**Is A$2,191 per week an entry-level electrician salary?**

No. It is a median for full-time, non-managerial adult-rate employees in the occupation group. It should not be presented as an apprentice or entry-level wage.

**What qualification is used for the Electrician (General) pathway?**

Training.gov.au lists UEE30820 Certificate III in Electrotechnology Electrician as a current Certificate III mapped to ANZSCO 341111 Electrician (General). Licensing and supervised-work requirements must still be checked separately.

**Do electricians need a licence in Australia?**

Electrical licensing is administered at state and territory level. For example, NSW requires an electrical licence or certificate for electrical wiring work, and Queensland states that electrical work without an appropriate licence is illegal except for limited authorised circumstances. Check the regulator where you intend to work.
