Ireland can be a strong option for students who want an English-speaking, EU-based study experience. But an English course is not interchangeable with a university programme, and the school, visa permission and work conditions need to be checked together before you pay a deposit.

This guide is designed for the question students actually need answered: **which Irish English course is suitable for my stay length and immigration plan?** It deliberately does not rank schools using copied review scores or publish a single “average” fee, because course prices, accommodation availability and provider status change frequently.

## Start with the right route

### A short English course

For a short visit, first check whether your passport requires a visa and what stay you are permitted to make. Do not assume that a visa-free tourist stay permits work or can be converted into a student route after arrival. The Irish Immigration Service publishes the current study options and visa requirements by nationality.

### An English course longer than 90 days

If you intend to study for more than 90 days, choose an English-language programme that appears on the **Interim List of Eligible Programmes (ILEP)** or is eligible through the newer **TrustEd Ireland** framework. The programme—not merely the school’s marketing—must be eligible for the permission you plan to seek.

Ireland is transitioning from the former ACELS-based system to TrustEd Ireland. QQI says that existing ACELS providers do not move across automatically; providers must apply and satisfy the new framework. ILEP remains relevant during the transition, so always verify the provider and the exact programme immediately before enrolling.

## If you are planning to use Stamp 2

For an eligible English-language course, Irish immigration guidance currently expects a programme of at least **25 weeks** with at least **15 hours of daytime tuition each week**. A student can generally take up to three English-language programmes, for a maximum of two years in total. Renewal is not automatic: attendance, an end-of-course examination and evidence of progression can matter.

Eligible Stamp 2 students may work up to **20 hours per week during term time** and up to **40 hours per week during specified holiday periods**. These are work-limit rules, not a promise of employment or an immigration pathway after a language course. An English course by itself does not create a right to live or work elsewhere in the EU.

<Callout type="warning" title="Do not pay based on an accreditation label alone">
Ask the provider for the exact programme name, start and end dates, weekly classroom hours, fee/refund terms, learner-protection arrangements and its current ILEP or TrustEd status. Verify each point against the official lists before applying for permission or making a non-refundable payment.
</Callout>

## How to choose a school: a five-point checklist

1. **Immigration eligibility.** Check the course on the official list if you need study permission. A school may offer both eligible and non-eligible courses.
2. **Teaching schedule.** Confirm classroom contact hours, level-testing process, class size policy and the exam/progression requirement in writing.
3. **Total cost, not weekly tuition alone.** Request a quote that separates tuition, registration, materials, accommodation, airport transfer, insurance and any exam fee.
4. **Refund and learner protection.** Read the refund timetable, cancellation conditions and what happens if the course changes or cannot proceed.
5. **City and housing.** Check current accommodation availability before choosing a city. A lower course fee can be outweighed by limited housing or a longer commute.

## Dublin, Cork, Galway or Limerick?

| City | A sensible reason to consider it | What to verify first |
|---|---|---|
| **Dublin** | The broadest choice of providers, international networks and transport connections | Housing availability, commute and the full accommodation quote |
| **Cork** | A smaller city option with a distinct student and cultural scene | Course start dates and whether the provider can arrange accommodation |
| **Galway** | A compact, cultural city that may suit students seeking a smaller setting | Seasonal accommodation supply and travel connections |
| **Limerick** | A smaller-city study environment and access to the west/mid-west | Provider choice, timetable and local housing options |

There is no reliable permanent “cheapest city” ranking. Accommodation supply, season and room type move too quickly. Compare dated, written quotes for the same course length and room standard.

## Build a realistic budget

Use your own quotes to fill this checklist before booking:

| Budget item | What to ask for |
|---|---|
| Tuition | Total course fee, not only a promotional weekly rate |
| School charges | Registration, materials, exam and administration fees |
| Accommodation | Deposit, rent, meals, utilities, move-in/out dates and cancellation terms |
| Immigration and insurance | Applicable visa/registration costs and health-insurance cover |
| Daily living | Transport, food, phone, study materials and contingency money |
| Travel | Flights, airport transfer and the cost of arriving before accommodation begins |

## Frequently asked questions

**Do I need a visa for an English course in Ireland?**

It depends on your nationality and the length and purpose of your stay. For study longer than 90 days, use the official Irish Immigration Service guidance and check the exact course’s eligibility before booking.

**Can I work while studying English in Ireland?**

Only an eligible student with the relevant Stamp 2 permission can use the student work concession. Current guidance allows up to 20 hours per week in term time and 40 hours in specified holiday periods. A short visit or a non-eligible course does not give those work rights.

**Is ACELS still the right accreditation to look for?**

Ireland is moving to TrustEd Ireland. Existing ACELS status does not automatically transfer, and ILEP remains part of the transition. Check the provider and programme against current official information rather than relying on an old badge or directory listing.

**Can an English course lead directly to long-term work or EU residence?**

No. A language course and student permission are temporary study arrangements. Work beyond the student concession and any long-term residence route require separate eligibility under Irish immigration law.
