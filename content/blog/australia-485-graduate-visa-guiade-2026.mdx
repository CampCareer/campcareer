The 485 visa is one of the biggest reasons many international students choose Australia.

It gives you time after graduation to stay in Australia, work full-time, build local experience, and decide whether the country still makes sense for your long-term future.

But here is the part many students miss: the 485 visa is not a guaranteed PR pathway. It is a temporary window. Used well, it can help you move toward skilled migration, employer sponsorship, or a stronger international career. Used poorly, it can become two years of expensive waiting.

This guide explains how the Australia Temporary Graduate visa subclass 485 works in 2026, who it is for, what changed, and how to think about it from a career and ROI perspective.

This article is general information only, not migration advice. Visa rules change often, so always check the Australian Department of Home Affairs before applying.

Quick answer: what is the 485 visa?

The Temporary Graduate visa subclass 485 is a temporary visa for eligible international students who have recently completed studies in Australia.

In simple terms, it lets you:

* stay in Australia after graduation
* work full-time
* gain local experience
* bring eligible family members
* prepare for a future visa pathway

For many students, this is the bridge between the Australia Student Visa subclass 500 and a longer-term career or migration plan.

Which 485 visa stream should you choose?

The 485 visa is not one single pathway. The stream matters.

1. Post-Higher Education Work stream

This is usually the main stream for students who completed a bachelor’s, master’s, or doctoral degree in Australia.

It is generally the right stream if your highest Australian qualification is:

* Bachelor degree
* Bachelor honours degree
* Master’s degree
* Doctoral degree

For most university graduates, this is the stream they think of when they say “post-study work visa in Australia.”

2. Post-Vocational Education Work stream

This stream is for graduates with vocational qualifications, such as:

* associate degree
* diploma
* trade qualification

The important difference is that your qualification normally needs to be relevant to an eligible occupation. This is where students can get caught. A diploma alone does not automatically mean the 485 visa is available.

If you are choosing between university and vocational study, do not only compare tuition fees. Compare the visa and career pathway too. A cheaper course can become expensive if it does not support your next step.

3. Second Post-Higher Education Work stream

This stream is for some graduates who already held a first Temporary Graduate visa and studied in a regional area.

It can give extra time, but it is not for everyone. Your eligibility depends on where you studied and where you lived during your first 485 visa.

Regional study can be useful, but it should not be chosen only because “regional sounds better for PR.” The course, occupation, salary, and job market still matter.

How long can you stay on a 485 visa?

The stay period depends on your stream, qualification, passport, and specific policy settings at the time you apply.

For many graduates, the broad pattern looks like this:

Qualification or stream	Typical stay period
Post-Vocational Education Work stream	Up to 18 months
Bachelor degree	Usually up to 2 years
Master’s by coursework or extended master’s	Usually up to 2 years
Master’s by research	Usually up to 3 years
Doctoral degree	Usually up to 3 years
Eligible Indian nationals under AI-ECTA	May receive different stay periods
Hong Kong or British National Overseas passport holders	May receive longer stay periods
Second Post-Higher Education Work stream	Additional 1–2 years in eligible cases

Do not plan your entire life around the maximum possible duration. Plan around the most conservative duration you are likely to receive.

A student who has two years after graduation needs a very different job strategy from someone who has three or four years.

Basic eligibility checklist

The exact rules depend on your stream, but in general, you should check these areas early.

1. Age

Most applicants must be 35 years old or under when applying.

There are limited exceptions for some groups, such as certain research graduates or specific passport holders. Do not assume the old age limit still applies.

This is especially important for students starting later in life. If you are 31, 32, or 33 before beginning a degree, the 485 visa timing needs to be checked before you enrol.

2. Australian study requirement

You generally need to have completed eligible Australian study.

This usually means your course must be CRICOS-registered and satisfy the Australian study requirement. One important part is that your study must have been completed in Australia over the required period.

Students sometimes focus only on the school name and forget the course structure. That can be risky if they transfer, package multiple courses, take long breaks, or study partly offshore.

3. Timing after completion

Timing is one of the most common problems.

Many students think the deadline starts from the graduation ceremony. It usually does not work that way. Visa timing can depend on when your course was completed, not when you walk across the stage.

Before your final semester ends, prepare:

* completion letter
* transcript
* English test result
* passport
* health insurance
* police check
* current visa details

Waiting until your student visa is about to expire is not a strategy.

4. English requirement

You need to meet the English requirement accepted for your visa subclass.

The frustrating part is that English test rules can change. Some tests are accepted only under specific conditions, and online or at-home versions may not be accepted for Australian visa purposes.

The safest approach is simple:

* check the accepted test list before booking
* take the test early
* make sure the result is valid on the day you apply
* do not rely on an old test result without checking validity

5. Health insurance

Your student health insurance may not be enough after your student visa period.

For the 485 visa, you need suitable health insurance arrangements. This is a small detail, but small details are exactly what delay applications.

6. Character and police check

You may need police checks and character documents.

Do this early. Police checks can take time, and an incomplete application can create unnecessary stress.

The biggest mistake: treating the 485 as “extra time”

The 485 visa is not just extra time in Australia. It is your career runway.

The problem is that many graduates spend the first six months resting, applying casually, or working unrelated casual jobs. By the time they start thinking seriously about skilled work, half the visa is gone.

A better way to use the 485 visa is to divide it into three phases.

A practical 485 visa career timeline

Final semester: prepare before you graduate

Before your course finishes, you should already know:

* which 485 stream you are likely to apply for
* when your student visa expires
* what English test you need
* whether your course supports your chosen pathway
* what graduate jobs open before graduation
* whether your target occupation needs a skills assessment later

Month 0–3 after completion: lodge correctly and start applying seriously

Your first goal is not to “relax after graduation.” Your first goal is to avoid visa mistakes and build employment momentum.

Focus on:

* lodging the correct visa application
* preparing a local-style CV
* applying to graduate roles
* improving LinkedIn
* contacting alumni
* applying to internships or junior roles
* building a portfolio if you are in tech, design, data, or marketing

For competitive fields like computer science, business, engineering, and health, the job search should begin before the 485 visa is granted.

Month 3–12: get local experience

This is where the 485 visa becomes valuable.

Your goal is to move from “international graduate” to “local candidate with Australian experience.”

That may mean:

* graduate program
* junior full-time role
* paid internship
* casual role inside your target industry
* contract work
* portfolio projects
* networking with employers

The first job does not have to be perfect. But it should move you closer to your target occupation.

Month 12–24: decide your next visa strategy

Do not wait until the last three months.

Around the middle of your 485 visa, you should already be checking:

* skilled migration options
* state nomination requirements
* employer sponsorship possibility
* salary level
* occupation list relevance
* English score improvement
* skills assessment requirements
* regional options
* whether staying in Australia still makes financial sense

Is the 485 visa worth it?

Usually, yes — but not always.

The 485 visa is most valuable when you have a realistic plan to convert Australian study into better career outcomes.

It is probably worth it if:

* your degree connects to strong job demand
* you can apply for skilled or graduate roles quickly
* your English is strong enough for employers and future visas
* you are willing to move cities if needed
* your salary potential can justify the study cost
* your occupation has a possible long-term pathway

It may be less valuable if:

* your degree has weak job outcomes
* you chose the course only because it was easy to enter
* you have no plan beyond “I will figure it out later”
* your English is not strong enough for interviews
* your expected salary is too low compared with tuition and living costs
* your occupation has limited migration options

485 visa and PR: how are they connected?

The 485 visa does not directly give you permanent residency.

But it can help you build the ingredients that some PR or longer-term visa pathways may need:

* Australian work experience
* better English score
* employer sponsorship opportunity
* state nomination readiness
* skills assessment preparation
* stronger salary and career evidence
* time to understand the labour market

Think of the 485 visa as a bridge, not the destination.

A strong 485 strategy looks like this:

Student visa → Graduation → 485 visa → Skilled job → Skills assessment / nomination / sponsorship → Longer-term visa option

A weak strategy looks like this:

Student visa → Graduation → 485 visa → Random work → Panic near expiry

The visa is the same. The outcome is different because the plan is different.

Common 485 visa mistakes

Mistake 1: applying too late

Do not wait until the final week. Gather documents before your course completion is confirmed.

Mistake 2: choosing the wrong stream

Degree graduates and vocational graduates are not treated the same. Check the stream carefully.

Mistake 3: assuming every Australian course leads to a 485 visa

Not every course creates the same post-study option. Course type, CRICOS registration, study length, qualification level, and occupation relevance can all matter.

Mistake 4: ignoring English test timing

Your English result needs to meet the visa rules at the time you apply. Do not assume a test from years ago is still usable.

Mistake 5: thinking the 485 visa guarantees PR

It does not. PR depends on many other factors, including occupation, points, sponsorship, state rules, work experience, and policy changes.

Mistake 6: not checking salary reality

A graduate visa is useful only if it helps you move toward better income or long-term opportunity. Before choosing a course, check whether graduates in your field can realistically earn enough to justify the total cost.

How to choose a course if the 485 visa matters to you

Students often choose a course in this order:

1. country
2. university
3. tuition fee
4. course name

But if your goal includes post-study work, skilled employment, or PR, a better order is:

1. target occupation
2. employability
3. salary potential
4. visa pathway
5. course quality
6. total cost
7. university brand

A famous university can help, but it does not fix a weak career plan. A cheaper course can save money, but it may cost more later if the job outcome is poor.

For Australia, the best decision is usually not the cheapest course or the highest-ranked university. It is the course that gives you the best combination of:

* realistic admission chance
* manageable cost
* strong employment outcome
* visa compatibility

Final thoughts

The 485 visa is one of Australia’s strongest advantages for international students, but only if you treat it seriously.

It gives you time. It does not give you a guaranteed job. It gives you work rights. It does not guarantee PR. It gives you a chance to prove yourself in the Australian labour market. That chance is valuable, but it needs a plan.

Before choosing a degree, ask three questions:

1. Can this course make me eligible for a useful post-study pathway?
2. Can I realistically get skilled work during the 485 period?
3. Will the salary after graduation justify the cost of studying?

FAQ

What is the Australia 485 visa?

The 485 visa is a temporary graduate visa for eligible international students who completed study in Australia. It allows graduates to stay, work, and study temporarily after finishing their course.

Does the 485 visa give full work rights?

In most cases, yes. The 485 visa is commonly used by graduates to work full-time and gain Australian work experience.

How long can I stay on a 485 visa?

It depends on your stream, qualification, passport, and eligibility. Many graduates receive between 18 months and 3 years, while some groups may receive longer periods.

Is the 485 visa a PR visa?

No. The 485 visa is temporary. However, it can help you gain work experience and prepare for skilled migration, employer sponsorship, or other future pathways.

Do I need a job offer for the 485 visa?

Usually, no. The 485 visa is not an employer-sponsored visa. However, getting a skilled job during the visa period is often important for your long-term plan.

Can I apply for another student visa after a 485 visa?

Australia has restricted some onshore visa switching pathways. Do not assume you can simply move from a 485 visa back to a student visa inside Australia. Check the latest rules before planning this.

Is the 485 visa worth it for international students?

It can be worth it if your degree has strong job outcomes and you use the visa period strategically. It is less useful if you have no career plan or if your course has weak employment prospects.
