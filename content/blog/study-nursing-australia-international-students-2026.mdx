Nursing is one of the clearest study-to-profession pathways in Australia—but only when the course is chosen correctly. The deciding question is not whether a provider calls its course “nursing.” It is whether the exact qualification, campus and delivery mode lead to the registration you need with the Nursing and Midwifery Board of Australia (NMBA).

For an international student aiming to become a **Registered Nurse (RN)**, the safest starting point is an NMBA-approved, CRICOS-listed pre-registration nursing degree. From there, the journey is: complete clinical education, apply for registration, enter the workforce, then assess any graduate-visa or skilled-migration options that apply to *your* circumstances.

## The short answer: which course should you choose?

| Your goal | Typical study route | Professional outcome to verify |
|---|---|---|
| Become a Registered Nurse | Bachelor of Nursing, usually three years full time | Eligibility to apply for **Registered Nurse** registration with NMBA |
| You already hold a non-nursing bachelor’s degree | A clearly identified pre-registration master’s program, if approved | Eligibility to apply for **Registered Nurse** registration with NMBA |
| Work as an Enrolled Nurse | Diploma of Nursing at an approved provider | Eligibility to apply for **Enrolled Nurse** registration with NMBA |
| Move from Enrolled Nurse to RN | A provider-specific articulated pathway into an approved bachelor’s degree | Do not assume credit transfer or registration outcome without written confirmation |

The word **“approved”** matters. AHPRA says graduates of approved programs are qualified to apply for registration, but must still apply and meet all other registration requirements. A course may also be CRICOS-listed for international students without leading to the particular registration outcome you expect. Check both lists.

## RN versus Enrolled Nurse: do not treat them as the same pathway

Australia has different nursing registration divisions. An approved bachelor’s or approved pre-registration master’s can lead toward Registered Nurse registration. A Diploma of Nursing can lead toward Enrolled Nurse registration, whose scope of practice is different and includes direct or indirect supervision by a registered nurse.

Neither route is “better” in every situation. The risk is choosing a diploma because it appears shorter or cheaper while assuming it produces the same RN outcome as a bachelor’s degree. It does not. Before accepting an offer, ask the provider—in writing—which NMBA registration division the exact CRICOS course and campus support.

## The five checks to make before paying a deposit

### 1. Check the exact course in AHPRA’s Approved Programs search

Search the provider, course title and campus in [AHPRA’s Approved Programs of Study tool](https://www.ahpra.gov.au/accreditation/approved-programs-of-study.aspx). Approval is course-specific. Do not rely only on a university’s general reputation or a course brochure from a previous intake.

### 2. Check CRICOS separately

International students need an offer for the correct CRICOS-registered course. Confirm the CRICOS code, campus, full-time duration, delivery mode and next intake on the provider’s official page or the [Study Australia course search](https://search.studyaustralia.gov.au/). A program can have different campus codes, start dates and fees.

### 3. Ask about clinical placement before you enrol

Nursing is not a classroom-only degree. Ask how placements are scheduled, whether evening/weekend blocks occur, which travel costs you must cover, what immunisations, police checks, Working with Children checks, first-aid certificates or uniforms may be required, and whether paid work can realistically fit around placement blocks. The answer affects your real budget more than a headline tuition fee.

### 4. Treat English and registration as a separate requirement

Finishing an approved course does not automatically grant registration. NMBA registration standards can include English-language, criminal-history, professional-indemnity and other checks. Your university admission English score and the English evidence accepted for professional registration may not be identical. Check the current NMBA registration standard before choosing an English test or assuming a prior qualification is enough.

### 5. Compare the total cost, not tuition alone

Build a dated provider-by-provider budget that separates:

- annual course tuition and compulsory student fees;
- OSHC for the whole student-visa period;
- placement travel, screening, vaccinations, uniforms and equipment;
- accommodation and transport for each campus or placement location; and
- a contingency fund for placement weeks when paid shifts may be harder to schedule.

Australia’s OSHC is normally required for the full period of a Student visa. It is not Medicare, and cover, exclusions and out-of-pocket costs differ by policy.

## What the labour-market data actually says

Registered Nurses are a large Australian occupation, but “high demand” should not be read as a guaranteed job, visa or PR outcome. Jobs and Skills Australia’s current profile reports 366,200 employed Registered Nurses, annual employment growth of 12,600, median weekly full-time earnings of A$2,192 and a 44% part-time share. The workforce is concentrated in Victoria (28.1%), New South Wales (26.5%), Queensland (22.5%) and Western Australia (10.9%).

That evidence is useful for deciding whether nursing is a serious career target. It is not enough by itself to choose a state, predict a graduate job offer or infer a future immigration invitation. Employer hiring, local health-service requirements, registration timing and state nomination settings all vary.

<Callout type="tip" title="Use labour-market data as a career signal, not a migration promise">
Nursing can be a strong choice if you genuinely want clinical, patient-facing work. Do not choose it only for a shortage label. Placements, shift work, communication standards and professional registration are core parts of the career—not administrative steps after graduation.
</Callout>

## After graduation: registration first, then work and visa planning

### Apply for registration

After an approved program, apply to the NMBA through AHPRA. Keep your completion documents, placement evidence and identity documents organised well before the course ends. Registration is the point that turns a nursing degree into eligibility to practise; a job application cannot replace it.

### Understand the 485 accurately

An eligible nursing degree can support an application for the Temporary Graduate visa (subclass 485), but nursing does not create a special 485 duration. Under the current Post-Higher Education Work stream, the standard stay period is two years for a bachelor’s or coursework/extended master’s degree and three years for a research master’s or doctoral degree. Age, Australian-study requirements, passport-specific rules and regional provisions can change individual eligibility.

### Keep PR as a separate decision

Nursing may be relevant to skilled-migration pathways, but permanent residence is not automatic after study, registration or employment. Skills assessments, state criteria, occupation lists, points, invitations and visa rules can change. Make a plan that still makes sense if your PR timing is longer than expected.

## A decision framework that prevents the common mistakes

| Question | Good evidence before you decide |
|---|---|
| Will this course lead to RN or Enrolled Nurse registration? | The exact course and campus appear in AHPRA’s Approved Programs search; the provider confirms the intended NMBA division |
| Can I afford the real programme? | A written fee quote plus placement, insurance, housing and travel budget |
| Can I complete placement requirements? | Written provider guidance on schedules, compliance checks and placement locations |
| Will this meet a graduate-visa requirement? | Your own qualification level, study duration, age and Home Affairs rules—not a provider slogan |
| Is nursing a good long-term career fit? | You understand patient contact, shift work, clinical accountability and registration obligations |

## Frequently asked questions

**Is a Bachelor of Nursing enough to become a Registered Nurse in Australia?**

An approved bachelor’s degree makes you qualified to apply for RN registration, but registration is a separate application. Confirm the exact program is approved and check NMBA’s current registration standards.

**Can a Diploma of Nursing make me a Registered Nurse?**

No. An approved Diploma of Nursing supports eligibility to apply for Enrolled Nurse registration. It is a distinct professional outcome. Some providers offer pathways into a bachelor’s degree, but credit and RN eligibility must be confirmed for the specific pathway.

**Is nursing a guaranteed PR pathway in Australia?**

No. Nursing can have strong employment relevance, but permanent-residence pathways depend on changing federal and state criteria, skills assessment, points, invitation settings and your own facts. Build a career plan that is worthwhile even without a guaranteed migration result.

**How much can Registered Nurses earn in Australia?**

JSA reports median full-time weekly earnings of A$2,192 before tax for Registered Nurses, based on ABS May 2025 data. Individual pay varies by state, employer, enterprise agreement, level, shift penalties and hours worked.

**Can I work while studying nursing?**

Student-visa work conditions and course placement schedules both matter. Check current visa conditions through Home Affairs, then ask the provider how placements affect paid-work availability. Do not budget on the assumption that every week will allow regular part-time shifts.
